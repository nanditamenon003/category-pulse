# Category Pulse

**A daily assistant for a clothing store's manager and floor team.** It tracks every product category against its monthly target, spots what's genuinely falling behind, works out *why* (sold out, missing sizes, fewer shoppers, fewer buyers, or just not selling), and says what to do about it while there's still time to act.

**Live app:** [category-pulse.streamlit.app](https://category-pulse.streamlit.app/) (choose **Look around first** to explore a sample store with no sign-up) · **Case study:** [CASE_STUDY.md](CASE_STUDY.md)

> **All data in this project is simulated.** No real store, sales or customer data is in the repository. The store model (lines, categories, monthly targets, size ranges) is based on how a real premium apparel store runs, but every number is generated by `src/generate_data.py` from a fixed random seed.

## Why a store manager would use it

- **The morning huddle, done.** **Tomorrow's plan** puts it on one page: what tomorrow needs to sell to keep the month on course, the few categories to focus on with one thing to do and what to say at the till, the sizes to request, and how many people to put on each floor at the busy hour. One tap shares it to the team's WhatsApp group.
- **The evening spreadsheet, replaced.** The end-of-day report is already calculated, checked and explained, as a short message for the area manager.
- **Problems caught days earlier, with the reason.** A category with plenty of stock but no 32 or 34 waists is flagged as a broken size run the day it happens, not a week later when the total finally drops. Stock that isn't selling is flagged too, with the money tied up in it.
- **It reads the reports the store already has.** Upload the till system's sales export as it comes: no template, no retyping.

## What it does

| Feature | What the manager sees |
|---|---|
| **Month-to-date status** | Every category in words and colour: Behind, Watch (slipping, not yet proven), On track, Ahead, or Too early to tell, shown as plainly as "21% behind · needs 7 a day". Only gaps bigger than normal day-to-day ups and downs count as "behind", so small categories don't raise false alarms. |
| **Why it's behind** | One likely cause with the evidence and one action: sold out, broken size run (the shelf looks full but the core sizes are gone), fewer visitors, fewer buyers, or a slow seller (weeks of stock, few buying). |
| **Stock** | Stockouts, broken size runs, last-piece alerts, what to request per size (enough until the delivery after next, with a margin on core sizes), sizes at risk of running out before the next delivery, and slow stock with the money tied up in it. |
| **Month-end range** | Where the month is likely to land, as a range with the chance of reaching target, not a single falsely precise number. |
| **Tomorrow's plan** | Goal, focus categories, stock to request, busy hours and people per floor, ready to share. |
| **Floor and staff** | In plain words: "12 in every 100 visitors bought, down from 21", what to check, busy times like "5-8 pm". |
| **Sell** | For each category that's behind, short steps for the till: what to show, what to suggest with it, and which offer each loyalty tier is likeliest to say yes to. |
| **Summary** | The evening report as a WhatsApp message, the full plain-English summary, and a contribution report that checks its own totals. |
| **AI chat** | Ask anything ("why is womenswear behind: traffic or conversion?"). The AI answers by calling the same tools, and a **"How I got this"** panel shows every lookup and number it used. |
| **Your own data** | Upload your store's exports in whatever shape they come; judge pace in units or rupees. See below. |

## Tested, not just claimed

Every forward-looking number is checked against what actually happened next (`tests/`):

| Claim | Result |
|---|---|
| The month-end range holds the middle 80% of outcomes | 77-87% of thousands of simulated months, ordinary and bumpy, finished inside it |
| "At risk of running out" is a fair watch list | Flagged sizes sold out before the next delivery 22% of the time, against 1.7% for other sizes, so the page calls it a watch list, not a certainty |
| Slow stock really is slow | The week after being flagged it sold 23% of its pieces, against 64% for stock in general |
| A raw till export is read with no help | Report title, bill lines, date and time in one cell, customer columns, a Grand Total row, last year mixed in: all read correctly, and personal columns never kept |

One idea failed its test and was left out: predicting the day (or the chance) each size sells out. At a few pieces per size per week, it did no better than guessing the average. The request quantities cover that risk with a margin instead. No machine-learning model is used yet, for the same reason: on one simulated month it would only re-learn the simulator. The tests above are ready to judge one on real data.

## The problem

In many stores, each category (polos, chinos, women's knit tops, kids' denim...) has a monthly unit target. Progress is checked by downloading a sales report from the store's POS/ERP system into a spreadsheet, usually at the end of the day. That has blind spots:

- **Problems are seen too late.** By the time the numbers are pulled, the selling day is over.
- **"Balance to do" misleads.** A raw gap to target says nothing about how much of the month is left, so a category that will comfortably finish can look worse than one in real trouble.
- **Size problems are invisible.** A category can have plenty of stock overall while the sizes most people wear are gone, so it quietly stops selling.
- **Manual spreadsheets break silently.** A formula range that misses a few rows gives wrong totals without any warning.
- **The "why" and "what now" are left to guesswork:** fewer shoppers, an empty shelf, missing sizes? Who should staff which floor tomorrow? What should associates offer at the till?

Category Pulse is a layer on top of the store's existing system, not a replacement. It turns the same numbers into timely, explained, actionable signals.

## Using your own store's data

Managers rarely have a tidy spreadsheet in a template's format, so the **Your data** page takes what they do have.

- **Only sales are required:** a date, a category and units sold, one row per bill line or per day. Rupee values, sizes, times and bill numbers each add more. Stock reports, targets, visitor counts and loyalty tier figures are optional extra files or sheets.
- **Any export works as it comes.** The app finds the table under a report title, works out whether each sheet is sales, stock, targets or visitors, and works out each column from the words shops use ("Qty", "Net Amt", "Bill No", "Sub Category", "SOH", "Target Value"...). A Men/Women column beside the real category becomes the floor. It drops Grand Total rows, takes each sale's hour from the bill time, counts bills, keeps the latest month, and reads a single stock report run the next morning as the previous close. The page shows **how it read each column**, with a dropdown to change any of it, and remembers the choices for next month's file.
- **Targets in whatever form they exist:** per category, per floor, per line or one number for the whole store, in units, rupees or both; typed on the upload page; or none. Floor and store targets are shared across categories by how each sold before the last week (so one slipping category still shows), and labelled as estimates. With no target, a category is judged against **last year's sales** for the month (read from the same file if it goes back a year) or, failing that, against **its own pace earlier in the month**. Every screen says which yardstick it's using.
- **Units or rupees:** the switch at the top judges every category's pace in units or rupees. Rupees are shown as shops say them: ₹45,000, ₹2.45 L, ₹1.20 Cr.
- Ordinary spreadsheet habits are fine: numbers like 1,200, times like 2 PM, names in different capitals, returns as negative units. Every problem in a file is listed at once, and anything a page can't show is explained ("Not in your data: stock counts..."), never guessed.
- The Excel template (with a *Read me* sheet) is still there for stores that would rather fill something in.
- **Saved to your account, privately:** only the columns in use are saved (customer names, phone numbers and anything else in an export are dropped first), encrypted by the app before they leave, stored against a one-way fingerprint of your login rather than your email, never shared or sold. **Delete my data** removes it completely. The **AI chat works on uploaded data only after a clear yes**: it explains that the figures it looks up are sent to its AI provider (DeepSeek) and asks the person to continue only with dummy data or approved figures.

## The Sample Store's month

The simulation is day 24 of a 31-day month. None of its problems is hard-coded as an outcome: they **emerge from supply and demand**, because the simulation can only sell what's on the shelf.

| Category | What happens | What Category Pulse finds |
|---|---|---|
| Women Tops | Its day-18 delivery never arrives | Sold out, with shoppers still coming (normal visitors, collapsed buying) and the missed delivery named |
| Men Casual Trousers ("chinos") | The warehouse is out of waists 32/34 all month | Broken size run: 60 units on the shelf, none in the sizes most people need |
| Men Formal Blazers | Sells slowly all month | Slow seller: 20 pieces, about 10 weeks' worth, ₹2.9 L tied up |
| Little Boys Tops | Sells well above plan | Ahead |
| Men Denim Jeans, Men Formal Shirts | Normal trading | On track |

## Running it

Requires **Python 3.11**.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/nanditamenon003/category-pulse.git
cd category-pulse
python -m venv .venv
```

Activate the environment (Windows: `.venv\Scripts\activate`, macOS/Linux: `source .venv/bin/activate`), then:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
streamlit run src/app.py
```

The app builds the Sample Store's data on first start (or run `python src/generate_data.py` yourself). Everything except the AI chat and sign-in works with no keys; without sign-in settings, the welcome page simply offers "Look around first".

**For sign-in**, create a free [Auth0](https://auth0.com) "Regular Web Application":
- Allowed Callback URLs and Allowed Logout URLs: `http://localhost:8501/oauth2callback` (plus your live app's address + `/oauth2callback`).
- In `.streamlit/secrets.toml` (never committed), add:

```toml
[auth]
redirect_uri = "http://localhost:8501/oauth2callback"
cookie_secret = "a long random string"

[auth.auth0]
client_id = "..."
client_secret = "..."
server_metadata_url = "https://YOUR-AUTH0-DOMAIN/.well-known/openid-configuration"
```

Auth0 handles creating accounts, passwords, "Continue with Google" and password resets; the app only receives the person's name and email.

**For saving data to accounts**, create a free [Supabase](https://supabase.com) project, run the SQL in `src/storage.py` (one table, row-level security on), and add to the same secrets file:

```toml
[storage]
supabase_url = "https://YOUR-PROJECT.supabase.co"
supabase_key = "the project's secret (service_role) key"
encryption_key = "generate once with: python -c \"from cryptography.fernet import Fernet; print(Fernet.generate_key().decode())\""
```

Use the same `encryption_key` everywhere the app runs, or data saved in one place can't be opened in another.

**For the AI chat**, copy `.env.example` to `.env` and add a key for the provider set in `src/config.py` (`AI_PROVIDER`):

- `"deepseek"` (current setting) needs `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY`
- `"anthropic"` (Claude, as the original spec intended) needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`

The chat code is the same for both: DeepSeek offers an Anthropic-compatible endpoint, so switching is one line.

Each module can also be run on its own and prints its own checks, e.g. `python src/kpi.py` (pace table and contribution report), `python src/stock.py`, `python src/forecast.py` (month-end ranges), `python src/plan.py` (tomorrow's plan), `python src/digest.py` (end-of-day summary and WhatsApp report), or `python src/agent.py` (terminal chat).

**Tests** (each runs on its own, e.g. `python tests/test_plan.py`):

- `test_any_store.py`: the whole engine on a made-up store that looks nothing like the Sample Store, and on a bare version with only daily sales; planted problems are found, missing data is reported plainly.
- `test_upload.py`: the sample file reads back exactly as the Sample Store; loosely named CSV files work; broken files are refused with a plain message.
- `test_smart_import.py`: a raw bill-level till export read with no help; floor targets in rupees; a typed store target; saved data reads back the same; changed column choices are remembered.
- `test_plan.py`: the month-end range is honest; the sold-out and broken-size categories come first in the plan and the request; a bare store gets a plan that says less rather than guessing.
- `test_slow_stock.py`: slow stock stays slow; the blazers are named a slow seller; the run-out watch list is fair.
- `test_storage.py`: saved data is scrambled at rest, visible only to its owner, and gone after delete.

### Deploying (Streamlit Community Cloud, free)

1. Sign in at [share.streamlit.io](https://share.streamlit.io) with GitHub and create an app from this repository, with the main file `src/app.py` and Python 3.11.
2. Under **Advanced settings → Secrets**, add `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY = "your-key"` and, below it, the `[auth]` and `[storage]` settings above, with `redirect_uri` set to the live address + `/oauth2callback`.
3. Deploy. The chat is for signed-in users only, with daily limits counted on the server (`QUESTIONS_PER_DAY` per account and `ALL_QUESTIONS_PER_DAY` for everyone, in `src/config.py`), so neither one visitor nor a busy day can use up the API credit.

## Design choices in brief

- **Every number comes from the data.** The AI must call tools before answering and may not estimate. Projections are always labelled as projections, and tested (see above).
- **Plain words for the floor.** "21% behind", "12 in every 100 visitors bought", "5-8 pm", "put 3 people on Menswear": written for the people who act on it, not for analysts.
- **Status before numbers.** Green, amber and red mean status only, always with a word label (so it works for colour-blind users). Buttons and links use one accent, the violet from the logo; the logo's full gradient is kept for brand touches, so it's never mistaken for a status. Built to be read on a phone.
- **Privacy by design.** Loyalty targeting works at tier level (Platinum, Gold, Silver, not a member); no individual customer data exists anywhere in the project. Only the columns in use are kept from an upload, encrypted when saved, and the AI sees a store's figures only after a clear yes.
- **Honest about gaps.** Every feature says plainly when the data it needs is missing, rather than guessing.

## Limitations

- The Sample Store is simulated. Uploads work for any store, one month at a time (the latest month in the file). Column guesses cover common retail wording; anything unusual can be set with the dropdowns, once.
- Uploaded data is read as at the close of its latest day. Stock alerts are only as detailed as the stock counts supplied (a daily count gives "by today's close", not the hour).
- When core sizes aren't given, they're learned from sales, which can pick the wrong sizes when sales are few. The upload page shows them so they can be checked.
- Projections assume the rest of the month keeps its pace. Targets shared out from a floor or store total, or taken from last year or a category's own earlier pace, are estimates, and the screens say so.
- On uploaded data, the AI chat sends the figures it looks up to DeepSeek (after asking), which suits dummy data and demos. A real-data pilot would need the store's approval and an AI provider the company approves. Each question is answered on its own (no conversation memory), in roughly 5 to 30 seconds.
